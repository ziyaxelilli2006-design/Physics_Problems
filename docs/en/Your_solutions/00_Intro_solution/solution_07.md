## 7. Logic & Series: The Fly and the Bicycle

### The Theory: Constant Velocity

The key to this problem is realizing that the fly is moving at a **constant speed** ($2\text{ m/s}$) for the entire duration of the event. To find the total distance, we don't need to know *where* the fly is going; we only need to know **how long** it is in the air.

**The Golden Formula:**


$$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$$

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation

**The Setup:**

* Distance to the wall: $10\text{ meters}$
* Bicycle speed: $1\text{ m/s}$
* Fly speed: $2\text{ m/s}$

#### Step 1: Calculate the Time until "Crunch"

The event ends when the bicycle reaches the wall and crushes the fly. Since the bicycle moves at a steady $1\text{ m/s}$ and has $10\text{ meters}$ to cover, we calculate the time ($t$):


$$t = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed of Bicycle}}$$

$$t = \frac{10\text{ m}}{1\text{ m/s}} = \mathbf{10\text{ seconds}}$$


The fly will be flying for exactly $10$ seconds.

#### Step 2: Calculate the Fly's Total Distance

Regardless of how many times the fly turns around or how complex its path is, it maintains a constant speed of $2\text{ m/s}$. We now apply that speed to the total time we just found:


$$\text{Distance}_{\text{fly}} = \text{Speed}_{\text{fly}} \times t$$

$$\text{Distance}_{\text{fly}} = 2\text{ m/s} \times 10\text{ s} = \mathbf{20\text{ meters}}$$

---

### Final Result

The fly travels a total distance of **$20\text{ meters}$** before being crushed.

### Why this is a "Logic" problem:

If you tried to solve this by adding up the first trip to the wall, then the trip back to the bike, then the trip back to the wall, you would be calculating an **infinite series**:


$$d = \text{trip}_1 + \text{trip}_2 + \text{trip}_3 + \dots$$


While the math works out to the same $20\text{ meters}$, it takes pages of calculations. The "Physicist's Trick" is to realize that **Time** is the bridge that connects the bicycle's movement to the fly's movement.
