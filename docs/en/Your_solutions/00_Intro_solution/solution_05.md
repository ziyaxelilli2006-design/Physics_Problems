

## 5. Trigonometry: Vector Components

### The Theory: SOH CAH TOA

When a vector is drawn at an angle, it forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. We use basic trigonometry to find the lengths of the "legs" of that triangle.

* **Hypotenuse ($|\vec{A}|$):** The total magnitude of the vector ($15$).
* **Adjacent side ($A_x$):** The horizontal component (along the $x$-axis).
* **Opposite side ($A_y$):** The vertical component (along the $y$-axis).

For any vector at an angle $\theta$ from the horizontal:

* **Cosine** relates the Adjacent side to the Hypotenuse: $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$
* **Sine** relates the Opposite side to the Hypotenuse: $\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation

**Given:**

* Magnitude $|\vec{A}| = 15$
* Angle $\theta = 60^\circ$

#### Step 1: Calculate the Horizontal Component ($A_x$)

Using the cosine relationship ($A_x = |\vec{A}| \cos \theta$):


$$A_x = 15 \cdot \cos(60^\circ)$$


From the unit circle, we know that $\cos(60^\circ) = 0.5$.


$$A_x = 15 \cdot 0.5 = \mathbf{7.5}$$

#### Step 2: Calculate the Vertical Component ($A_y$)

Using the sine relationship ($A_y = |\vec{A}| \sin \theta$):


$$A_y = 15 \cdot \sin(60^\circ)$$


From the unit circle, we know that $\sin(60^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866$.


$$A_y = 15 \cdot 0.866 \approx \mathbf{12.99}$$

---

### Final Result

The vector $\vec{A}$ can be written in component form as:


$$\vec{A} = [7.5, 12.99]$$

**Physical Intuition:** If you were pulling a sled with a rope at a $60^\circ$ angle with a force of $15$ Newtons, only $7.5$ Newtons of that force is actually pulling the sled forward ($A_x$). The other $12.99$ Newtons of force is actually lifting the sled upward ($A_y$). Because the angle is steep ($60^\circ$), more of your effort is going into lifting than pulling forward!
