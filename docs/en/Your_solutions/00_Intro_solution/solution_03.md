
## 3. Proportionality: The Universal Law of Gravitation

### The Theory: The Inverse-Square Law

Isaac Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation is expressed as:


$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

* **$F$ (Force):** The pull between two objects.
* **$m_1, m_2$ (Masses):** The amount of matter in the objects. Force is **directly proportional** to the product of the masses. If you double a mass, you double the force.
* **$r$ (Distance):** The distance between centers. Force is **inversely proportional to the square** of the distance. This is the "Inverse-Square Law."
* **$G$:** The gravitational constant (a fixed number that doesn't change).

---

### The Problem: Changing the Inputs

We are asked to find the factor by which the force $F$ changes if:

1. Both masses ($m_1$ and $m_2$) are **halved** ($\times 1/2$).
2. The distance ($r$) is **doubled** ($\times 2$).

### Step-by-Step Scaling Analysis

#### Step 1: Analyze the Masses (Numerator)

Since $F \propto m_1 \cdot m_2$:

* Mass 1 becomes $\frac{1}{2}m_1$
* Mass 2 becomes $\frac{1}{2}m_2$
* Combined effect: $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}}$
* *Theory:* Reducing mass reduces the "source" of the gravity. Because both are halved, the numerator of our fraction is now 4 times smaller.

#### Step 2: Analyze the Distance (Denominator)

Since $F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$:

* Distance $r$ becomes $2r$.
* Because the distance is **squared**, we must square the change: $(2)^2 = 4$.
* Since it is in the **denominator**, this acts as a $1/4$ factor.
* *Theory:* Moving objects further apart weakens gravity rapidly. Doubling the distance makes the force 4 times weaker.

#### Step 3: Calculate the Total Factor

Now, we multiply the two scaling factors together:


$$\text{Total Factor} = (\text{Mass Factor}) \times (\text{Distance Factor})$$

$$\text{Total Factor} = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{16}}$$

---

### Final Result

The force $F$ changes by a factor of **$1/16$**. This means the new gravitational pull is 16 times weaker than the original.

**Physical Intuition:** If you were standing on a planet and suddenly the planet lost 75% of its mass (the masses effect) and you were moved twice as far from its center (the distance effect), you would feel almost weightless—feeling only $1/16$th of the gravity you felt before.
